Mobile physical therapy

Can physical therapy come to your home?

Yes. Mobile outpatient PT brings one-on-one care into the environment where you actually move, practice, and live.

Kind Oasis Integrative HealthUpdated September 20264-minute read

Physical therapy does not have to happen in a traditional clinic. The American Physical Therapy Association notes that PT can be provided in homes, workplaces, schools, fitness settings, and communities as well as outpatient clinics.

For some people, the home is simply more convenient. For others, it is the most useful place to understand why a movement is difficult and practice a solution that fits.

Mobile outpatient PT is not automatically the same as home health. Eligibility, coverage, and documentation can differ. Ask the practice and your insurance plan how a proposed service is classified and covered.

What can an in-home visit include?

A mobile PT visit can include many of the same core elements as clinic-based care: an evaluation, individualized exercise, movement practice, manual therapy when appropriate, education, and a plan for between visits.

The setting also makes it possible to work directly on real-life tasks, such as:

  • Getting up from your usual chair or bed.
  • Managing the steps you use every day.
  • Walking on the surfaces inside and outside your home.
  • Finding safe, realistic places to complete exercises.
  • Building strength and endurance for errands, hobbies, or community activities.

Who may benefit?

Mobile PT may be useful after surgery or injury, with balance or mobility changes, when pain makes travel difficult, or when the goal itself is tied closely to the home. It can also help people who prefer a quieter one-on-one setting.

The right location depends on your goals, medical needs, safety, and coverage. A brief screening can help determine whether mobile outpatient PT, telehealth, home health, or another setting is the best fit.

What should you ask before starting?

  • Is the service outpatient PT, home health, or another type of care?
  • Does the therapist serve my location?
  • What will my insurance plan cover, and what might I owe?
  • What space or equipment should I have ready?
  • How will progress be measured?
